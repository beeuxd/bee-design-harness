# Pitch deck

A deck is a narrative with evidence, read in under 3 minutes without you in the room. The
slides exist to carry one argument: *this is inevitable, and this team is why it happens now.*

## House rules

- **Never invent a metric.** No placeholder traction, no "industry standard" conversion rates,
  no synthesized market sizes. Missing number → flag it and leave a visibly marked gap for the
  founder to fill. A deck with honest gaps beats one with confident fiction.
- **Every claim carries its evidence** — a real user quote, a chart from real data, a signed
  LOI. The harness's traceability instinct applies here: assertion → source.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a paragraph, it's two slides or it's cut.
- Visual treatment comes after the narrative locks: hand the locked outline to
  `ppt-visual-design` (and `brandkit` if identity doesn't exist yet).

## The narrative spine (10–12 slides)

1. **Title** — company, one-line what-we-do a stranger understands, contact.
2. **Problem** — whose pain, how acute, evidenced with a real quote or datum. Not "X is broken."
3. **Why now** — the shift (tech, regulation, behavior) that makes this possible today and not
   five years ago. Weakest slide in most decks; strongest lever in good ones.
4. **Solution** — what you built, shown not described. Product image beats architecture diagram.
5. **Traction** — the chart that goes up. Pick the *one* metric that proves the thesis; cohort or
   retention beats vanity totals. Pre-traction: strongest evidence of pull (waitlist, pilots, LOIs).
6. **Market** — bottom-up (who pays what, how many of them), not a Gartner TAM circle.
7. **Business model** — who pays, unit economics as known today, honestly labeled as early.
8. **Competition** — a real map that concedes competitors' strengths and names your wedge.
   Never the two-axis chart where you're alone in the top right of axes you chose.
9. **Team** — why *this* team is unfair for *this* problem. Earned secrets over logos.
10. **Ask** — amount, runway it buys, and the 2–3 milestones it funds (what will be true at the
    next raise).
11–12. **Appendix** — the detail you hope they ask about: deeper metrics, roadmap, financial model.

## Stage calibration

Pre-seed/seed: the argument is team × insight × early pull — slides 2, 3, 9 carry the deck.
Series A: the argument is repeatability — slide 5 carries it; show the machine, not just growth.

## Reviewing an existing deck

Walk it as a cold reader: (1) after 30 seconds, can you say what they do and why now?
(2) find every unevidenced claim and every invented-looking number; (3) check the spine order —
most broken decks bury traction or open with market; (4) cut count: decks lose slides in review,
never gain them. Deliver findings with severity, per slide.
